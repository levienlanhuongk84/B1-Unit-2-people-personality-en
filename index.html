Sheet 4 / 8 · Functional language

Chunks that link ideas

10 functions · 30 chunks

1 · Giving opinions

  • If you ask me, …
  • My honest opinion is that …
  • From what I've seen, …

If you ask me, a friendly face matters as much as a steady hand. From what I've seen, patients relax the moment a doctor smiles and asks how they really are.

2 · Likes & dislikes

  • I really enjoy + ‑ing …
  • What I like most about … is …
  • I'm not a big fan of …

What I like most about my job is meeting so many different people, although I'm not a big fan of the paperwork afterwards.

3 · Wants & plans

  • I'd really like to …
  • I'm hoping to … / I'm planning to …
  • One day, I'd love to …

One day, I'd love to work in a hospital in the mountains, and I'm hoping to start applying for rural placements next year.

4 · Describing people

  • He's got … and he's (quite) …
  • She's the kind of person who …
  • What I like about him is that he's …

She's the kind of person who remembers every patient's name, and what I like about her is that she's never too busy to listen.

5 · Giving reasons

  • One big reason is that …
  • That's because …
  • …, which is why …

Nurses spend more time with patients, which is why their personality matters so much — one big reason is that patients remember how they were treated long after they forget the medicine.

6 · Giving examples

  • A good example of this is …
  • Like … , for instance
  • You can really see this in …

A good example of this is my old professor — strict in class, but you can really see this in the way he still calls his former students to check how they're doing.

7 · Contrast & concession

  • However, … / Although …, …
  • Then again, …
  • Even so, …

Although shy doctors can seem cold at first, then again many of them turn out to be the most careful listeners on the ward.

8 · Inviting & suggesting

  • Would you like to come to … ?
  • Why don't we … ?
  • Let's … , shall we?

Why don't we take Dr Sarah to the Old Quarter on Sunday? Let's ask if she's free, shall we?

9 · Replying to invitations

  • Thanks for asking — I'd be happy to come.
  • It's very kind of you, but …
  • Maybe next time?

It's very kind of you, but I've already agreed to cover the evening shift — maybe next time?

10 · Concluding & evaluating

  • All in all, … / Overall, …
  • Weighing it all up, …
  • That's why I believe …

Weighing it all up, I'd rather work with a kind colleague than a brilliant but rude one — that's why I believe personality should count in every interview.

Answer frame — 7 steps

Use it for Speaking Part 2, Part 3 and every body paragraph of an essay. Sample question: "What makes someone a good colleague?"

1Point

For me, the most important quality in a colleague is being reliable.

2Why

That's because in a hospital we depend on each other every minute.

3How

A reliable colleague hands over patients carefully and does what they promise.

4Example

For instance, my friend Lan always double-checks the drug chart before she goes home.

5Result

As a result, the whole team trusts her and fewer mistakes happen.

6Limitation

Admittedly, very careful people can be a little slow.

7Evaluation

On balance, though, I'd rather work with someone slow and reliable than fast and careless.

Tips
  • Part 1: Point + Why (+ Example) is enough.
  • Part 2: all 7 steps in 2 minutes.
  • Part 3 and essay: stress Limitation + Evaluation — this is what lifts an answer above B1.

PointThe colleague I really admire is Nga, the most experienced nurse in my department. I first met her on my very first night shift, about eight years ago, when I was a nervous young doctor. WhyI admire her because she's so caring and patient. She's the kind of person who always has time for a frightened relative, even at three in the morning.

HowShe's in her early fifties, average height and quite slim. She's got short hair that's going grey, but she has a warm smile that makes her look much younger. In her free time she enjoys gardening, and she often brings us vegetables from her garden.

ExampleOne night, a young mother came in with a very sick baby and started shouting at everyone. I didn't know what to do, but Nga sat down next to the mother, held her hand and spoke quietly. LimitationAdmittedly, she can be quite strict with junior staff, especially about handovers. EvaluationBut all in all, she taught me that kindness is part of good medicine, and I'd like to be like her one day.

Task 2 · Essay — opinion

Prompt

Some people believe that a doctor's personality is just as important as his or her medical knowledge.

To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Give reasons and examples. Write at least 250 words.

4–5-paragraph outline

  • Body 1 — reason 1 (70–90): patients: a caring, patient doctor can put patients at ease → they tell the truth → a better diagnosis; one example from the ward.
  • Body 2 — reason 2 (70–90): teamwork: get on well with nurses, keep calm under pressure, never lose your temper → fewer mistakes; one real example.

Model answer · Task 2 (≈ 274 words)

Most people agree that a good doctor needs years of study and a lot of medical knowledge. However, some people believe that a doctor's personality is just as important. Personally, I think this view is completely right, because patients need a doctor they can trust and talk to openly.

The main reason is that a caring doctor gets better information from patients. Many patients, especially older people from the countryside, look worried and say very little when they first arrive. A doctor who is patient and has a warm smile can put them at ease, so they explain their problems honestly. For example, on my ward last year, an elderly man only mentioned his chest pain after a young doctor had chatted with him for ten minutes. Thanks to her, his heart condition was treated in time.

Another reason is that hospital work is teamwork. Doctors have to get on well with nurses, colleagues and relatives, often during long night shifts. Doctors who keep calm under pressure make fewer mistakes. For instance, a brilliant surgeon I once worked with often lost his temper, so junior staff stopped asking him questions, and a drug error went unnoticed for two days. Admittedly, a kind doctor with poor knowledge can also cause harm, but hospitals can test knowledge with exams, while character is much harder to test.

All in all, I strongly agree that the way a doctor treats people is as important as what he or she knows. Knowledge helps a doctor choose the right treatment, but a caring, calm manner helps patients accept it and get better. That is why medical schools should teach both.
